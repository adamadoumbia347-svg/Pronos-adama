Which camera to watch your dog or cat? Our comparison

📹 Dog & cat · Buying guide · 7 min read · Updated October 20, 2026

Has your neighbour told you your dog barks when you leave? Does your cat seem stressed when you get home? A camera lets you see what really happens while you're away. It's often the first step to understanding a problem and choosing the right solution.

In short: to watch your pet, an indoor pan-and-tilt Wi-Fi camera with night vision and sound detection is enough in most cases. Cameras with a treat dispenser cost more: they're useful if you also want to reward your pet remotely.

Why film your pet?

What a pet does when alone is often different from what we imagine. Filming your departures tells you:

  • when they start barking or meowing, and how long it lasts;
  • how they behave: sleeping, getting bored, pacing, scratching at the door;
  • whether your efforts are working: the video shows progress week after week.

It's essential for working with a dog who barks when left alone, and very useful to show your vet if you consult them.

What to look for

  • Field of view: a pan-and-tilt camera (one that rotates) covers a whole room; a fixed camera needs to be well placed.
  • Night vision: essential if your pet stays in a dark room or in the evening.
  • Alerts: motion and sound (barking) detection so you're notified on your phone.
  • Recording: on a microSD card (no subscription) or in the cloud (often paid). Check what's free before you buy.
  • Privacy: choose a well-known brand, an account protected by a strong password, and a camera you can switch off when you're at home.
  • Wi-Fi: many cameras only work on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi; check your router.

1. The fixed Wi-Fi camera

Placed on a piece of furniture, it films one angle of the room. It's the simplest solution for a studio flat or if your pet always stays in the same spot. Put it up high, facing where they rest or the front door.

2. The pan-and-tilt camera: the best all-rounder

You rotate it remotely from your phone and it can follow your pet's movements. A single camera is often enough for a large room. Choose a model with sound detection so you're alerted to barking or meowing.

3. The treat-dispensing camera

As well as filming, it tosses a treat when you tap your phone. Furbo and Petcube are well-known brands of this type. It's handy for rewarding a calm dog while you're out, as part of gradual training. Be careful: tossing a treat just to “comfort” them when they bark risks rewarding the barking.

4. The microphone feature: use with care

Most cameras let you talk to your pet. Some pets find it reassuring, but many are confused by hearing your voice without seeing you: they look for you, get restless or bark even more. Test it first while you're in another room, and watch their reaction before using it remotely.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Watching without acting: the camera is there to help you understand, then to follow a real training plan.
  • Choosing without checking the subscription: some features (history, smart alerts) are paid.
  • Placing the camera too low: your pet may knock it over or chew the cable. Secure cables out of reach.
  • Using an anti-bark collar as well: it's a punishment that doesn't address the cause. Why we advise against it.
Key takeaways: a pan-and-tilt Wi-Fi camera with night vision and sound alerts is enough to understand what your pet does alone. The treat dispenser is a bonus, useful only if it rewards calm behaviour.
