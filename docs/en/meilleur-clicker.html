Which clicker should you choose to train your pet?

🐕🐈 All species · Buying guide · 5 min read · Updated October 16, 2026

The clicker is one of the cheapest and most effective training tools. But they're not all the same: a sound that's too loud can scare a cat, a stiff button tires your hand. Here's how to choose yours.

In short: for a dog, a button clicker with a wrist strap works very well. For a cat, a rabbit or a fearful animal, choose a soft-sounding or adjustable-volume clicker. For birds and targeting, a clicker + target stick kit is the most practical.

Reminder: what is a clicker for?

The click is a “marker”: it tells the animal the exact moment it does the right thing, and announces a reward. Its strength is its precision and consistency: the sound is always the same, unlike your voice. The whole method is explained in our guide to clicker training.

1. The classic “box” clicker

This is the original model: a small box with a metal tongue. Its loud click carries well outdoors. On the other hand, it can make sensitive animals jump. If so, start by holding it away from you or muffling it in a pocket.

2. The button clicker: the most versatile

The button is easy to press with one finger without looking, which helps you click at the right moment. The sound is a little softer than the classic box. The i-Click, designed by the team of Karen Pryor, one of the pioneers of clicker training, is a well-known model of this type.

Some models offer several sound levels. It's the right choice for a cat, a rabbit, a small rodent or a fearful dog: start at the lowest volume, then turn it up if needed for outdoor work.

4. The clicker + target stick kit

The stick (often telescopic, with a ball on the end) is used for targeting: the animal learns to touch the ball, then to follow it. Essential for birds, and very useful for cats, rabbits and agility tricks.

Which clicker for which pet?

  • Confident adult dog: button or classic box.
  • Puppy or fearful dog: button or adjustable volume, starting at a distance.
  • Cat: adjustable volume or a soft button; some cats prefer a short verbal marker.
  • Rabbit, rat, ferret: adjustable volume, on the lowest setting.
  • Parrot, budgie, cockatiel: clicker + target stick kit.

Get started in 3 steps

  1. Charge the clicker: click, then treat, 10 to 15 times, without asking for anything.
  2. Check: when you click, your pet turns towards you expecting its reward.
  3. Click the right behaviour: start with something simple, like touching your hand, sitting or looking you in the eye, then reward every click.

Keep the rewards within reach: a treat pouch makes sessions much smoother.

Avoid: clicking without rewarding, clicking to call your pet or to tell it off, and clicking next to a sensitive animal's ear. The click must never become an unpleasant noise.
Key points: the model matters less than consistency. Choose a sound that suits your pet's sensitivity, a shape that's comfortable in your hand, and keep sessions short, 3 to 5 minutes.
