🐶 30 days with my puppy: the training programme

One small exercise a day, 5 to 10 minutes, to lay the foundations gently: their name, toilet training, sit, recall, the lead… Tick each successful day: your progress is saved on this device, no sign-up.

0 / 30 days

No pressure: if an exercise is hard, do it again the next day. Every puppy learns at their own pace.

Week 1 · Settling in and trust

  1. Say their name in a happy voice; as soon as they look at you, a treat. 5 times, 3 times a day.

  2. Set up their bed somewhere quiet. Drop a treat or a chew there when they lie down on their own. Read more

  3. Take them out when they wake up, after every meal, after play and every 1 to 2 hours. Praise them outside, at that very moment. Read more

  4. Touch a paw, an ear, look at their teeth for one second, then a treat. Stop if they tense up. Read more

  5. Leave the room for 10 seconds and come back calmly, before they cry. Build up very gradually. Read more

  6. Give them a suitable chew toy; if they nibble your hands, swap them for the toy. Read more

  7. A day with nothing new: calm games, cuddles, sleep. Puppies sleep a lot, that's normal.

Week 2 · First basics

  1. Hold a treat above their head: as soon as their bottom touches the floor, "yes!" and a treat. Read more

  2. Reward every time they look you in the eyes on their own. It's the foundation of attention. Read more

  3. Hold your open hand near their nose: when they touch it with their nose, a treat. Read more

  4. From the sit, lower the treat between their paws, then forward along the floor. Read more

  5. Show the harness, treat. Put it on for one second, treat. Then fasten it for a few seconds, at home. Read more

  6. Click then give a treat, 10 times: they learn that the click means a reward is coming. Read more

  7. Practise their name, sit and the hand target in 3 one-minute mini-sessions.

Week 3 · Discovering the world

  1. Play everyday sounds at low volume (vacuum, doorbell) while you play with them. Stop if they're scared. Read more

  2. Let them watch calm people from a distance and reward them. No forced contact. Before vaccinations are complete, ask your vet for advice. Read more

  3. At home or in the garden: every time the lead is loose and they're near you, a treat. Read more

  4. Call them in a happy voice from 2 metres, celebrate and treat when they arrive. Never call them for something unpleasant. Read more

  5. Hide a few pieces of kibble in a snuffle mat or under cups: they search with their nose. Read more

  6. Settle them in the car with the engine off, with a treat, for a few minutes. Then a very short trip.

  7. A quiet walk where they sniff at their own pace: it's very tiring mentally.

Week 4 · Consolidating

  1. Sitting, count 1 second, reward, then release them with a word ("okay"). Build up very slowly. Read more

  2. A treat in your closed fist: as soon as they stop trying to get it, a treat from the other hand. Read more

  3. Reward every time they lie down on a mat. Little by little, the mat becomes their place to settle. Read more

  4. One stroke of the brush, one treat. Very short, very pleasant, so they enjoy it. Read more

  5. In a safe place, with a long line: call them, reward generously, then let them go back to playing. Read more

  6. If your vet agrees, drop in to say hello: treats and cuddles, no treatment.

  7. With a stuffed chew toy, leave for a few minutes and come back calmly. They should stay relaxed. Read more

  8. Sit, look at me and recall, once each on a walk, with a great reward.

  9. 30 days of foundations. Keep going a few minutes a day: training lasts a lifetime, in a good mood. Read more

Programme rules: very short sessions, always with rewards, never punishment. Stop before they get bored. For outings and meetings before vaccinations are complete, follow your vet's advice.
