Which scratching post should you choose for your cat? Our comparison

🐈 Cat · Buying guide · 6 min read · Updated October 16, 2026

Plenty of scratching posts end up ignored in a corner while the cat carries on attacking the sofa. The problem rarely lies with the cat: it's usually the post that doesn't suit it. Here's how to choose one your cat will really use.

In short: for a first scratching post, choose a tall (at least 70–80 cm), heavy and stable sisal post, and add a flat cardboard scratcher if your cat scratches rugs.

The 4 criteria that really matter

  1. Stability. A post that wobbles or tips over scares the cat, who won't come back. This is the number one criterion.
  2. Height. Your cat must be able to stretch out fully, front legs extended: allow at least 70 to 80 cm for an adult cat.
  3. Material. Woven sisal (rope or fabric) is the favourite. Cardboard is very popular for scratching flat. Carpet is less advisable: it looks just like your rugs.
  4. Direction. Some cats scratch vertically (sofa, doors), others horizontally (rugs, doormats). Watch yours before you buy.

1. The sisal post: the first thing to buy

A simple post, as long as it's tall and weighted, covers most needs: scent marking, claw care and stretching. Put it right in front of the spot your cat is currently damaging.

For an indoor cat, a cat tree offers much more than a scratching post: height to watch from, hiding places to rest and a way to burn off energy. Choose thick posts, a wide base and, for tall trees, a wall fixing.

4. The sloped scratcher: for older cats and kittens

Halfway between vertical and horizontal, it's easy to use for a cat with stiff joints or a kitten discovering scratching. It complements a post nicely.

How many scratching posts do you need?

At least one per cat, and ideally one near each important area: next to where your cat sleeps (cats scratch a lot when they wake up), near the entrance to the main room and in front of the targeted furniture. In a multi-cat home, add more to avoid tension.

Getting your cat to use the post

A new scratching post doesn't smell of your cat. To help it get adopted: rub a little catnip on it, run a feather wand over it, and reward every scratch. That's positive reinforcement. Meanwhile, temporarily cover the forbidden area (managing the environment).

Avoid: small lightweight posts that tip over, carpet identical to your rugs, and a post hidden in a corner your cat never visits. Never punish a cat for scratching: it doesn't understand and becomes fearful.
Key points: stable, tall, sisal and well placed. Watch whether your cat scratches vertically or horizontally, and plan for at least one scratching post per cat.
