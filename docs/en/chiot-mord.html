My puppy bites: what should I do?

🐶 Puppy · Beginner · 5 min read

Scratched hands, holey socks, children who are scared… Puppy biting is exhausting, but it's normal. With the right habits, it decreases noticeably within a few weeks.

Why puppies bite

  • They explore with their mouths, just as a baby touches everything with its hands.
  • They are teething, roughly between 3 and 6 months: chewing brings relief.
  • They play the way they did with their littermates.
  • They are tired or over-excited: the most underestimated cause.

1. Teach it to soften its bite

Between puppies, a little yelp stops the game when a bite is too hard. Do the same: as soon as teeth press down, stop moving, calmly withdraw your hand and stop all interaction for 10 to 20 seconds. Then resume the game. It learns that biting hard makes the human disappear.

2. Always offer an alternative

Keep chew toys within reach at all times. As soon as your puppy targets your hands: offer a toy instead. When it chews the toy: praise. This is the principle of ignoring + rewarding the alternative.

3. Respect its naps

A puppy needs 16 to 20 hours of sleep a day. A puppy biting frantically at the end of the day is often an exhausted puppy. Settle it somewhere quiet in its own corner with a chew toy: it often falls asleep within minutes.

4. Channel its energy

Replace some hand games with sniffing games: hide kibble in a snuffle mat. Sniffing is mentally tiring and calming, much more than running.

5. Protect the children

Children run and shout, which excites the puppy. Teach them to become “a statue” if the puppy nips, and never leave them alone together. A safety gate helps separate spaces at lively moments.

Avoid: tapping the muzzle, holding the mouth shut, pinning the puppy to the ground. These gestures are frightening, can damage your relationship and increase the risk of real bites later. If biting comes with stiff growling, a frozen body or continues after 6 months, ask your vet for advice.
Key takeaways: stop the game when it presses too hard, offer a toy, respect nap time and channel energy through sniffing. Patience does the rest.
