🍎 Can my dog or cat eat…?

A banana, a piece of cheese, leftover chicken? Here, food by food, is what your dog and cat can eat, in moderation or never. Type a food to find it straight away.

Emergency: if your pet has eaten something toxic (chocolate, grapes, onion, xylitol…), call your vet or a pet poison helpline immediately, even if they seem fine. Don't try to make them vomit without veterinary advice.

The list, food by food

🍫 Chocolate

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Toxic: it contains theobromine. The darker it is, the more dangerous.

🍇 Grapes and raisins

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Can cause serious kidney damage in dogs, even in small amounts. Avoid for cats too.

🧅 Onion, shallot, leek

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Toxic raw, cooked or powdered (soups, sauces): damages red blood cells. Cats are even more sensitive.

🧄 Garlic and chives

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Same family as onion, same danger for the blood.

🍬 Xylitol (gum, "sugar-free" sweets)

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Very dangerous for dogs (blood sugar crash, liver). Check labels, including peanut butter.

🥑 Avocado

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Avoid: very fatty, can upset the gut, and the stone can block the intestine.

☕ Coffee, tea, energy drinks

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Caffeine is toxic to dogs and cats.

🍺 Alcohol

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Toxic, even in small amounts. Watch out for desserts containing alcohol too.

🌰 Macadamia and other nuts

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Macadamia nuts are toxic to dogs; other nuts are very fatty and sometimes mouldy. Avoid.

🫓 Raw dough (bread, pizza)

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

The yeast keeps rising in the stomach and produces alcohol. Dangerous.

🦴 Cooked bones

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

They splinter into shards that can injure the mouth, stomach or intestine.

🥓 Processed meats, fat and table scraps

🐕 Dog : ❌ Avoid 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Too salty and too fatty: risk of digestive upset and pancreatitis.

🍑 Peach, apricot, cherry

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

The flesh, in small amounts, without the stone: stones can block the intestine and contain toxic substances.

🍅 Tomato

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Dog: a little ripe tomato. Never the green parts (stems, leaves, unripe fruit). Cat: avoid.

🍊 Orange and citrus

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Dog: one or two segments without peel or seeds. Cat: avoid, they dislike it and the peel irritates them.

🥛 Cow's milk

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Many adult dogs and cats digest lactose poorly (diarrhoea). The cat's "saucer of milk" is a bad idea.

🧀 Cheese

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

A very small amount as a treat: fatty, salty and high in lactose.

🥣 Plain yoghurt

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

Plain, with no sugar or sweetener, in small amounts.

🥜 Peanut butter

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

Only xylitol-free, with no added salt or sugar, and in small amounts (very fatty). Pointless for cats.

🍞 Baked bread

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

A small piece of plain bread is fine, but adds nothing. Never with raisins, seeds or garlic.

🍝 Cooked pasta

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

Plain, without sauce, in small amounts.

🥔 Potato

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

Cooked and plain only. Never raw, green or sprouted.

🌽 Corn

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

A few plain kernels. Never the cob: it can block the intestine.

🥫 Canned tuna

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

Occasionally, in water (not oil or brine). Not as a regular meal.

🥣 The other species' food

🐕 Dog : ⚠️ In moderation 🐈 Cat : ❌ Avoid

Cat food is too rich for a dog. Dog food doesn't meet a cat's needs (taurine in particular).

🍗 Cooked chicken or turkey

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ✅ Yes

An excellent treat: cooked, plain, boneless, skinless and unseasoned.

🐟 Cooked fish

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ✅ Yes

Salmon, cod… cooked, plain and boneless.

🥚 Cooked egg

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ✅ Yes

Hard-boiled or scrambled without fat or salt. Not raw.

🎃 Cooked pumpkin and squash

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ✅ Yes

Cooked, plain, without sugar or spices: in small amounts.

🍚 Cooked rice

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

Plain and well cooked. Cats, as carnivores, don't really need it.

🥕 Carrot

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

Raw or cooked for dogs, in pieces suited to their size. For cats, cooked and in tiny pieces.

🫛 Green beans

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

Plain, without salt or butter.

🥒 Courgette and cucumber

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

Low in calories and full of water: a light treat for dogs.

🍎 Apple and pear

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

In small pieces, without core or seeds.

🍌 Banana

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

A few slices: it's sugary, so in moderation.

🍉 Watermelon and melon

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

The flesh only, without seeds or rind.

🫐 Blueberries and strawberries

🐕 Dog : ✅ Yes 🐈 Cat : ⚠️ In moderation

A few berries, washed, as a treat.

No food found.

The golden rules

  • Always plain: no salt, sugar, butter, sauce, spices, onion or garlic.
  • In small amounts: vets generally advise that treats should not exceed about 10% of what the animal eats in a day.
  • One new food at a time, in a small amount, to check they digest it well.
  • Cats are strict carnivores: fruit and vegetables are of no use to them. A little cooked meat or fish is better.
  • Allergy, illness, special diet: always ask your vet before giving human food.

Sources: toxic food lists from the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center and veterinary association guidance. This page does not replace your vet's advice.
