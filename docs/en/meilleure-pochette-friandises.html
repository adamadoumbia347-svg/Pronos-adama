Which treat pouch to choose? The comparison

🐕 Dog & puppy · Buying guide · 6 min read · Updated October 26, 2026

In training, everything happens in a second: the reward must arrive while your dog is doing what you want. Digging in a pocket or a bag is often too late. A well-chosen treat pouch changes everything. Here are the different types and their advantages.

In short: for most owners, a clip-on or belt pouch that opens and closes with one hand is enough. Choose a washable one, with a small extra pocket for poo bags and the clicker. A waist bag suits you if you carry more on walks.

What to look for

  • One-handed opening: the key criterion. You often hold the lead in your other hand. Rigid or magnetic openings stay open during the exercise and close with one gesture.
  • Closing: when you bend down or run, treats must not fall out. Otherwise your dog mostly learns to stare at your feet.
  • Washing: treats leave grease and smells. A removable lining or a silicone model is much easier to clean.
  • Attachment: clip on your belt or pocket, adjustable belt, or carabiner. Check it stays put when you move.
  • Extra pockets: for poo bags, the clicker, keys, phone.

1. The clip-on or belt pouch

The most common model: a small pouch with a rigid opening that clips onto your belt or trouser pocket. It opens with a press and stays open during the session. Ideal for exercises at home and short walks.

Pouch and clicker: a practical pair

If you use a clicker, choose a pouch with a ring or small pocket to attach it. The click marks the right behaviour at the exact moment, and the treat follows straight from the pouch. With both to hand, your sessions flow better and your dog understands faster what earns a reward. Our comparison which clicker to choose helps you find the right model.

Using it well

  • Prepare the session: fill the pouch with treats already cut, in a suitable amount.
  • Keep your hands empty between rewards: your dog learns to do the exercise, not to follow your hand. That's the whole point of the pouch, especially when you phase out the lure.
  • Wash it often, especially with moist treats.
  • Store it out of reach: many dogs know exactly where the treats are.
Common mistakes: a pouch that doesn't close properly (treats on the ground), a model too small for your fingers, or a pouch that hangs too low and bangs against your leg. Before buying, look at how it opens and how it attaches.
Key points: what matters is a pouch that opens and closes with one hand and is easy to wash. Clip-on pouch for everyday, waist bag for long walks, silicone for moist treats.
