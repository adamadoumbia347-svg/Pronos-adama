Cat scratching the sofa: 6 solutions that work

🐈 Cat · Beginner · 5 min read

Your cat isn't scratching the sofa to annoy you. Scratching is a vital need. The goal isn't to stop it, but to offer a better place to do it.

Why cats scratch

  • To mark their territory: glands between the paw pads leave scent, and the marks are a visual message.
  • To look after their claws by shedding the old outer sheaths.
  • To stretch their whole body, often on waking.

The sofa is perfect for all of this: stable, tall, well placed in the living room and full of your scent.

1. A scratching post more attractive than the sofa

The ideal scratching post is stable (it mustn't wobble), tall enough for your cat to stretch out fully (at least 70-80 cm) and covered in a material it can shred, such as sisal. Small posts that tip over are often ignored.

2. Put it in the right place

Place it right in front of the damaged part of the sofa, not in a hidden corner. Once the habit is formed, you can move it a few centimetres a day. Add another one near where your cat sleeps: cats scratch a lot when they wake up.

3. Add a horizontal one

Some cats prefer to scratch flat surfaces (rugs, doormats). Watch yours: if it goes for the bottom of the sofa or the rug, add a cardboard scratcher on the floor.

4. Make it irresistible

Rub some catnip on it, drag a feather wand around and over it so your cat sinks its claws in, and reward (treat, stroke) every time it uses it. That's positive reinforcement.

🌿

5. Make the sofa less appealing (temporarily)

For a few weeks, cover the scratched area with a throw or double-sided tape, which cats don't like touching. That's managing the environment: your cat stops reinforcing the habit in the wrong place while it learns the right one.

6. Look after its claws

Regularly trimmed claws do less damage. Get your cat used to it gently with cooperative care: one claw a day, one treat each time.

Never: shout at, spray or punish your cat. It won't understand the link with the sofa and will become fearful. Declawing (removing the last bones of the toes) is a mutilation, banned in the UK and in many European countries except for medical reasons.
Key takeaways: a tall, stable scratching post in front of the sofa, made attractive and rewarded, and the sofa covered while your cat learns.
