About Éduc'Animaux

Éduc'Animaux is a free, independent guide that brings together, in one place, kind and effective training methods for dogs, cats, rabbits, rodents, ferrets and birds.

Our mission

To help every owner understand their pet better and teach it good habits, with clear, step-by-step explanations and no jargon. A relationship built on trust makes life easier for the animal and for the whole family.

How we work

  • Reward-based methods. We recommend positive reinforcement, clicker training, targeting, managing the environment and desensitisation: the approaches supported by animal behaviour research and veterinary associations.
  • Never pain or fear. We advise against choke, prong and electric collars, physical punishment and “dominance” theory, which rest on outdated ideas and can harm an animal's welfare.
  • Health first. A sudden change in behaviour (new house-soiling, aggression, loss of appetite…) can have a medical cause, so we always refer you to a vet. Our advice does not replace theirs.
  • No miracle promises. Every animal learns at its own pace; we never invent figures or guaranteed results.

How our articles are written

Our articles are based on the sources listed below and follow strict writing rules: kind methods only, accuracy, and a referral to the vet for any health question. Each article shows when it was last updated. If you spot a mistake, we will correct it.

How the site is funded

The site is free and ad-free. It is funded through affiliate links: if you buy a product after clicking one of our links, we may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you. No manufacturer pays to be mentioned, and we only recommend gear that is genuinely useful for the method explained.

Our sources

Our advice draws in particular on:

  • American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior (AVSAB), Position Statement on Humane Dog Training, 2021. avsab.org
  • AVSAB, Position Statement on the Use of Dominance Theory in Behavior Modification of Animals, 2008. avsab.org
  • Ziv G., « The effects of using aversive training methods in dogs – A review », Journal of Veterinary Behavior, 19, 2017, p. 50-60.
  • Vieira de Castro A. C. et al., « Does training method matter? Evidence for the negative impact of aversive-based methods on companion dog welfare », PLOS ONE, 15(12), 2020.
  • China L., Mills D. S., Cooper J. J., « Efficacy of Dog Training With and Without Remote Electronic Collars vs. a Focus on Positive Reinforcement », Frontiers in Veterinary Science, 7, 2020.
  • Ellis S. L. H. et al., « AAFP and ISFM Feline Environmental Needs Guidelines », Journal of Feline Medicine and Surgery, 15(3), 2013, p. 219-230.
  • International Cat Care, resources on cat behaviour. icatcare.org
  • Pryor K., Don't Shoot the Dog: The Art of Teaching and Training (reference book on clicker training).
