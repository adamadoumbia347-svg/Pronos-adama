🐈 Training your cat (yes, it's possible!)

Cats learn very well… as long as you use rewards, very short sessions and plenty of patience. Punishment, on the other hand, doesn't work at all with them.

The 3 golden rules with a cat: 1 to 3-minute sessions, very tasty rewards (wet food, lickable treats) and never any force.

🪣 The litter tray

  • Rule of thumb: one tray per cat plus one, in quiet rooms.
  • Away from food and water bowls.
  • Cleaned every day; many cats prefer an open tray and fine, unscented litter.
  • Sudden house-soiling = a visit to the vet (urinary infection, stress).

💅 Scratching

Scratching is a vital need (marking, claw care, stretching). You don't stop it, you redirect it:

  1. Place a tall, stable scratching post right in front of the damaged area.
  2. Make it attractive: catnip, play with a wand toy around it.
  3. Reward every scratch in the right place.
  4. Temporarily make the forbidden area less pleasant (a throw, double-sided tape).

📣 Coming when called

  1. Pair a sound (its name, a treat packet rustle, a click) with a favourite treat.
  2. Call it when it is 1 metre away, reward. Repeat.
  3. Increase the distance, then call from another room.

Very handy for finding a hiding cat or bringing it in at night.

🛒 Lickable treats (top reward)

🧳 Loving the carrier

  1. Leave the carrier open in the living room all the time, with a blanket.
  2. Drop treats in it at random: it becomes a pleasant place.
  3. Feed meals in it, then close the door for a few seconds.
  4. Lift it a few centimetres, then take short trips.

🔘 Clicker training a cat

Cats love the clicker. Easy tricks to start with:

  • Touch a target with the nose (targeting).
  • Sit by capturing.
  • Paw, high five, jump onto a stool.

Choose a soft-sounding clicker: some cats are sensitive to noise.

🎣 Hunting, biting and energy

A cat that ambushes your ankles is often bored. Give it 2 hunting play sessions a day (10 min) with a feather wand, ending with a “prey” treat.

Never play with your hands: it would learn they are prey.
