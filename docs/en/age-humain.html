🎂 How old is your pet in human years?

Enter its age: we work out the human equivalent and give you tips suited to its life stage.

How is it calculated?

We use the most common estimate: a dog's or cat's first year equals about 15 human years, the second about 9 more, then each year counts for 4 to 6 years depending on species and size (large dogs age faster). It's only an estimate: breed, health and lifestyle matter too.

Why the "1 year = 7 years" rule is wrong

People often say one dog year equals seven human years. In reality, dogs and cats grow up very fast at first: at one year old they are already the equivalent of teenagers, and at two, young adults. After that, ageing slows down. In dogs, size matters too: large dogs age faster than small ones and are considered seniors earlier.

Life stages and their needs

  • Puppy or kitten (under one year): the ideal time for socialisation and good habits. Very short training sessions, lots of play and rest.
  • Young (1 to 3 years): lots of energy and sometimes a "teenage phase". The time to strengthen recall and loose-lead walking.
  • Adult: routine sets in. Keep activities that work their brain (sniffing games, puzzle toys) and keep an eye on their weight.
  • Senior: around 7 for most dogs (earlier for large breeds), around 11 for cats. Adapt exercise, offer a comfortable bed and talk to your vet about how often to schedule check-ups.

Stiffness, changes in appetite, thirst or behaviour, disorientation: at any age, an unusual change is worth a visit to the vet. For training at every age, see our dog guide and our cat guide.
