Which cat litter should you choose? The comparison

🐈 Cat · Buying guide · 6 min read

Clumping, plant-based, silica, clay… The shelves are full of cat litters, and the choice matters more than you might think. A litter your cat dislikes can be enough for them to go elsewhere. Here's how to choose, with your cat in mind first.

In short: most cats prefer a litter that is fine, soft under the paws and unscented. A clumping litter (clay or plant-based) is the easiest to keep clean. If your cat uses their current litter well, don't change it without a reason.

What to look for

  • Your cat's preference: the number one criterion. Many cats like a fine, sand-like grain.
  • Unscented: a cat's sense of smell is far more sensitive than ours. A strong fragrance can put them off.
  • Clumping: a litter that forms clumps lets you remove urine every day and keeps the box clean for longer.
  • Dust: choose a low-dust litter, for their sake and yours.
  • Weight and disposal: some plant-based litters are lighter. Check the packaging for how to dispose of it: not all can be flushed or composted.

1. Clumping clay litter

Made from clay, it forms firm clumps when wet. Its fine grain resembles the sand cats naturally like. It's often the best starting point, especially for a cat who pees outside the box. Choose it unscented and low-dust.

2. Plant-based litter

Made from wood, corn, wheat or tofu, it's lighter to carry, and many versions clump. Large, hard wood pellets don't suit every cat: fine-grain versions are often better accepted.

3. Silica litter

Its crystals absorb urine and limit odours. You remove poo daily, but urine stays in the box until a full change. Some cats dislike the hard grains or the crackling sound they make: watch how your cat reacts.

Switching litter without upsetting them

  1. Gradually: mix the new litter into the old one, increasing the proportion over one to two weeks.
  2. Or let them choose: set up a second box with the new litter next to the old one. They'll show you their preference.
  3. Watch: if they hesitate, scratch around the box or go next to it, go back to the previous litter.

Looking after the litter box

  • Scoop poo and urine clumps at least once a day.
  • Keep about 5 cm (2 inches) of litter in the box: enough to dig and cover.
  • Empty and wash the box regularly with hot water and mild soap, without scented products or bleach.
  • Have one box per cat, plus one.
