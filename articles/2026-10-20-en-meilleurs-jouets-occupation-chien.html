Which enrichment toys for a dog left alone? Our comparison

🐕 Dog · Buying guide · 7 min read

A bored dog will look for something to do… sometimes with your shoes, or by barking. Enrichment toys give them a calm, rewarding activity: licking, chewing, searching for their food. But not all of them are suitable when your dog is on their own. Here's how to choose.

In short: for a dog that stays home alone, the best choice is a tough rubber stuffable toy, filled with part of their daily food. Lick mats and snuffle mats are excellent too, but offer them under supervision until you know whether your dog chews them.

Why enrichment toys help

Licking, chewing and sniffing are natural activities that keep a dog busy and help them settle. They take mental effort, which is often more tiring than we think. Linked to your departure, a stuffed toy can also make that moment more pleasant: it's one of the tools in our method for the dog that barks when left alone.

A word of caution: a toy alone won't solve genuine distress about being left alone. If your dog won't touch their favourite treat when you leave, it's a sign they're too stressed to eat. Talk to your vet about it.

What to look for

  • Safety when alone: can your dog damage it and swallow pieces? This is the number one criterion.
  • Size: big enough that it can't be swallowed, and suited to your dog's jaw.
  • Difficulty: too easy and it's over in no time; too hard and your dog gives up. Start simple.
  • Cleaning: is it dishwasher-safe? Wet food dries quickly in the nooks and crannies.

1. The stuffable toy: the best for time alone

Hollow and made of thick rubber, it can be filled with wet food, soaked kibble or another suitable food. Your dog licks and chews it to get the contents out. After a few hours in the freezer, it lasts much longer. The Kong Classic is the best-known model of this type; it comes in several sizes and strength levels.

2. The lick mat: to calm

You spread food into its grooves and your dog licks it for a long time. Licking has a calming effect, which is very useful during grooming, a thunderstorm or just before you leave. Go for a thick silicone model with suction cups that is dishwasher-safe.

3. The snuffle mat: for the nose

Kibble hides between strips of fabric, and your dog has to find it with their nose. It's one of the most mentally tiring activities, perfect for rainy days. As it's made of fabric, keep it for times when you're around.

5. The puzzle toy: one to do together

Your dog pushes, lifts or slides pieces to find the reward. It's a great game to share, but the pieces can be chewed: don't leave it lying around. Start with the easiest level so your dog succeeds quickly.

Avoid when your dog is alone: soft toys and squeaky toys (pieces get swallowed), frayed ropes, cooked bones that can splinter, and any toy smaller than their mouth. If your dog swallows a piece, call your vet.

Getting the most out of them

  • Rotate the toys: put them away after use; novelty makes them more appealing.
  • Introduce them while you're there, with an easy filling, before leaving them when you go out.
  • Clean them after every use, especially with wet food.
  • They don't replace walks: a dog that has had a good walk and sniff before you leave settles more easily.
Key takeaways: for a dog left alone, rely on a sturdy stuffable toy that suits their size. Keep lick mats, snuffle mats and puzzles for shared moments, and remember to rotate them.
