Welcoming a puppy: the complete checklist (and what to skip)

🐶 Puppy · Buying guide · 7 min read

A puppy's arrival takes some preparation. Having the right supplies from day one prevents mischief, makes house training easier and reassures your puppy. Here's the complete checklist: the essentials, what can wait, and what you can skip buying.

In short: the 6 essentials are a bed or crate, bowls, the same food as at the breeder's or the shelter, a lightweight harness and a lead, chew toys and an enzymatic cleaner for accidents.

Before you shop: puppy-proof your home

Put electric cables, toxic plants, cleaning products, shoes and anything chewable out of reach. Set up a safe space for the times you can't supervise your puppy: it's the basis of managing the environment, and it saves you from having to scold.

1. A playpen or a gate

Marks out a safe corner where your puppy can play and rest without getting into mischief.

What can wait a few weeks

  • A 5 to 10 m long line, to work on recall outdoors once the vaccinations are done.
  • A permanent bed sized for adulthood: your puppy may well chew the first one.
  • A brush suited to their coat and nail clippers, introduced very gently, with treats.
  • A car safety harness, if you often travel with your puppy.

Pointless purchases (or ones to avoid)

  • Puppy pads as a long-term solution: they can help in a pinch, but they teach your puppy to go indoors. It's better to take them out often.
  • Choke, prong or shock collars: unnecessary and harmful for puppies and adult dogs alike. Why we advise against them.
  • A mountain of toys: 4 or 5 toys that you rotate each week are enough, and they keep the novelty alive.
Health: book a vet visit in the first few days (vaccinations, worming, feeding advice). Until the vaccinations are complete, ask your vet how to socialise your puppy safely.
Key takeaways: a safe space, something to eat, chew and rest on, a lightweight harness, treats and an enzymatic cleaner: with this kit, you're ready to welcome your puppy calmly.
